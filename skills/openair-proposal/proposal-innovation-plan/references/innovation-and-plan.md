# Innovation, Work Plan, and Expected Outcomes Method

This stage turns the completed research design into defensible innovation claims
and an executable implementation plan.

## 1. Distill innovation

Innovation should arise from the completed rationale, objectives, and methods.
For each major innovation:

- name the existing limitation or comparison boundary;
- state the proposed difference in question, mechanism, method, integration, or validation;
- explain why that difference enables new evidence or capability;
- identify the corresponding work package and evaluation;
- avoid absolute priority claims unless verified by the literature evidence.

Prefer a small set of distinct, defensible innovations over many overlapping
slogans. Do not repeat the method description and relabel it as innovation.

## 2. Build the implementation plan

Divide the project by logical milestones rather than equal calendar slices. Each
period should contain:

- the work packages and dependencies addressed;
- concrete activities;
- a reviewable milestone or intermediate artifact;
- a decision or contingency point;
- the evidence needed before proceeding.

Earlier periods commonly establish foundations and the first mechanism; middle
periods develop and validate major methods; later periods integrate, stress-test,
and consolidate results. Adapt this pattern to the actual project.

## 3. Define expected outcomes

Separate outcome categories when applicable:

- theoretical or empirical findings;
- methods, algorithms, datasets, tools, prototypes, or standards;
- publications, intellectual property, demonstrations, or training outcomes;
- reusable artifacts and reproducibility materials.

Outcomes must be observable at project completion and proportionate to duration,
team, and resources. Do not infer fixed publication counts, venues, patents, or
performance values without author confirmation.

## 4. Maintain section boundaries

Write innovation, milestones, annual schedule, expected outcomes, and
contingency points in `sections/07-plan.typ`.

Research foundation, available conditions, team support, and feasibility belong
to `sections/06-feasibility.typ`. Method details and equations remain
in the methods stage.

## 5. Quality gate

Confirm that:

- innovations are supported by the literature and technical route;
- each innovation has a distinct comparison boundary;
- milestones map to objectives and produce reviewable evidence;
- dependencies and contingency points are explicit;
- expected outcomes are specific but not fabricated or guaranteed.
