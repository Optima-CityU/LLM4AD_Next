# Objectives, Research Content, and Key Problems Method

This stage converts the established gap into a coherent objective hierarchy and a bounded set of research problems.

## 1. Define the objective hierarchy

Write one overall objective that identifies the target setting, central problem, intended research capability, and expected form of evidence. Then derive a small set of sub-objectives.

Each sub-objective must:

- describe an outcome rather than an activity;
- be attributable to a research content or work package;
- name the condition or constraint under which it applies;
- have evidence that can later distinguish progress from failure;
- remain achievable under the duration and resources in the foundation.

Avoid goals such as "study X", "explore Y", or "improve performance" without specifying the intended knowledge or capability.

## 2. Organize research content

For each work package, state:

1. the problem context and why it belongs in the proposal;
2. the research object, variables, or system boundary;
3. what relationship, mechanism, method, or artifact will be investigated;
4. what evidence or deliverable the package should produce;
5. dependencies on other work packages.

The packages should form a meaningful progression, such as understanding to method to validation, rather than a loose list of techniques.

## 3. Distill key scientific or technical problems

A key problem is the hard question inside a work package, not a renamed task. Develop each through five layers:

1. **Real setting:** the concrete phenomenon, limitation, or need.
2. **Special difficulty:** why standard assumptions or adjacent solutions do not directly apply.
3. **Existing evidence:** what verified literature or preliminary material already establishes.
4. **Core mechanism or contradiction:** the relationship that must be explained, controlled, or optimized.
5. **Research question:** a precise "how", "under what conditions", or "through what mechanism" question.

Include constraints in the question. Do not presuppose the method-stage answer.

## 4. Enforce correspondence

Maintain an internal one-to-one-or-explicit-many mapping:

`literature limitation -> sub-objective -> work package -> key problem -> expected evidence`

If one item has no counterpart, repair the objective structure within this stage. Do not edit the literature or rationale files; report any upstream inconsistency in the result summary so the author can return to that stage.

## 5. Keep content distinct from methods

This stage may identify the class of method or evidence needed, but it must not write detailed algorithms, equations, experiment protocols, or implementation steps. Those belong to the methods stage.

## 6. Quality gate

Confirm that:

- the overall objective answers the gap identified earlier;
- sub-objectives are neither duplicates nor unrelated expansions;
- every work package contributes directly to at least one objective;
- each key problem explains a genuine difficulty rather than restating content;
- scope exclusions and success evidence are explicit;
- terminology is consistent with the foundation and evidence base.
