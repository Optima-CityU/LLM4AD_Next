# Research Foundation and Feasibility Method

This stage establishes whether the applicant and project conditions can support
the already-defined research plan. It owns achieved facts and feasibility
reasoning, not innovation or future work planning.

## 1. Document the research foundation

Use only author-provided or source-grounded facts. Organize them by direct
relevance to the proposed work:

- prior findings, publications, prototypes, datasets, or engineering experience;
- theoretical, methodological, implementation, or experimental capabilities;
- available facilities, platforms, data access, collaborations, and institutional support;
- team roles and complementary expertise.

For each item, explain what project risk it reduces or what planned task it
enables. Do not turn a general biography or equipment inventory into evidence of
feasibility.

## 2. Analyze feasibility

Assess feasibility across the dimensions actually relevant to the project:

1. **Goal and scope:** whether objectives are bounded and mutually consistent.
2. **Technical route:** whether methods follow from prior evidence and have viable fallbacks.
3. **Research basis:** whether the team has the needed knowledge and preliminary work.
4. **Resources and access:** whether data, equipment, infrastructure, participants, or collaborators are genuinely available.
5. **Schedule:** whether dependencies and review points fit the project duration.
6. **Risk controls:** what happens if a method, resource, or assumption fails.

Every feasibility assertion requires a concrete basis. Ask the author for missing
consequential facts rather than filling them with plausible boilerplate.

## 3. Maintain section boundaries

Write research foundation, conditions, team support, feasibility analysis, and
risk controls in `sections/06-feasibility.typ`.

Innovation, milestones, annual schedules, and expected outcomes belong to
`sections/07-plan.typ`. Do not rewrite earlier claims to make
feasibility appear stronger; report mismatches so the author can return to the
owning stage.

## 4. Quality gate

Confirm that:

- achieved work and future work are unmistakably separated;
- every method has the resources, expertise, time, and fallback it needs;
- every feasibility claim names its concrete basis;
- risk controls are actionable rather than generic assurances;
- all names, results, facilities, and team facts are source-grounded.
